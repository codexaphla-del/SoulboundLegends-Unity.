SOULBOUND LEGENDS MASTER GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT Complete Game Concept,
Player Journey, World Rules, Progression, Loot, Dungeons, Records and
Rewards

DOCUMENT STATUS This document consolidates established Soulbound Legends
decisions and supplies clearly labelled proposed specifications where
exact values or tables have not yet been finalized. Proposed values are
design recommendations, not previously approved canon.

============================================================ 1. GAME
IDENTITY AND CORE VISION
============================================================

Soulbound Legends is a premium fantasy multiplayer mobile game built
around exploration, character growth, dangerous creatures, competitive
combat, kingdoms, dungeons, rare inheritances and a world-wide
progression story.

The experience should feel like a living fantasy world rather than a
collection of disconnected menus. Players begin as inexperienced
adventurers, learn the world, develop their characters, hunt
increasingly dangerous beasts, collect equipment and materials, join
other players, enter dungeons and eventually participate in conflicts
that affect the fate of the world.

The visual target is high-quality, detailed fantasy presentation:
expressive characters, believable armor and clothing, distinctive
weapons, large creatures, rich environments, castles, villages, ruins,
magical effects and readable mobile interfaces. The game must have its
own visual identity and must not copy another game’s protected
characters, maps, interface artwork or assets.

Core principles: - Exploration and discovery should be meaningful. -
Character progression should reward play, not only spending. - Rare
drops should feel exciting without making ordinary progression
impossible. - Every major activity should have a purpose and
corresponding rewards. - Competitive modes should be fair and separately
ranked. - The world should support future content updates without
rebuilding the entire game. - Server authorization should control
important accounts, purchases, trades, auctions and progression. -
Players should be able to understand why they received an item,
achievement or record.

============================================================ 2. THE
WORLD’S LORE
============================================================

The world’s creation and history are tied to ten elemental gods: Water,
Fire, Earth, Void, Thunder, Nature, Light, Air, Ice and the Omnigod.

There is also a hidden Shadow God. The Shadow God’s betrayal is not
revealed in the opening sequence. The opening should preserve mystery
rather than explain the entire story immediately.

A cinematic opening concept: 1. Show the vast fantasy world, its
kingdoms and the divine realm above it. 2. Introduce the ten elemental
gods seated or standing in a powerful celestial setting. 3. Show the
demonic threat and the war that endangers the world. 4. Show the gods
combining their power to create an enormous magical barrier in the sky.
5. Show the gods falling and losing their physical forms. 6. Leave the
true cause of the catastrophe and the Shadow God’s betrayal concealed.
7. Present the Soulbound Legends title. 8. Transition to the loading
screen and player onboarding.

The barrier is a major world-story mechanism. Its eventual fall is tied
to the claiming of all divine inheritances and the required
world-average level condition. The exact numerical average, server-wide
calculation, grace period and announcement rules must be finalized
before implementation.

God inheritances: - Supreme Ruins contain a route to a God
inheritance. - Only one challenger may attempt the relevant inheritance
challenge at a time. - A current holder cannot challenge for another
inheritance or assist another challenger in that restricted challenge. -
Rift events appear around cities and kingdoms. - Inheritance ownership
should be recorded permanently in world history and player records.

The world story should advance through server events, discoveries,
quests, environmental changes and major updates.

============================================================ 3.
FIRST-TIME PLAYER EXPERIENCE
============================================================

A new player should experience the following sequence:

1.  Opening cinematic The player sees the gods, the ancient war, the
    barrier and the ruined world. The hidden betrayal remains a mystery.

2.  Loading screen Display Soulbound Legends artwork, useful gameplay
    tips, event notices and loading progress.

3.  Account sign-in Supported options: Google, Apple where supported,
    and email. Account recovery and account security must be available.

4.  Player name The player enters an in-game name. Changing it later
    requires a rename card, subject to name availability and moderation
    rules.

5.  Character creation One-time initial character customization includes
    gender/body presentation, head, hair, eyes, ears and limited
    starting clothing. Male and female options control the appropriate
    character base and animation setup. More customization can be
    unlocked later.

6.  Beginner lobby The player enters at Level 0. The lobby introduces
    the main buttons, character, world access and basic navigation.

7.  Tutorial Teach movement, camera control, basic attacks, dodging,
    interaction, inventory, equipment, quests and safe beast hunting.
    Tutorials should be skippable only where doing so will not leave
    essential systems unexplained.

8.  Adventure World Before Level 10, Adventure World is the main world
    progression route. The player learns, explores, gathers, fights and
    completes beginner quests.

9.  Level 10 milestone Competitive modes and the relevant paid/privilege
    progression features remain locked until the Level 10 requirement is
    met. The Village-to-City/Kingdom access pass requirement applies as
    previously specified. The precise relationship between a pass,
    access eligibility and optional purchases must be communicated
    clearly; purchases must not silently grant competitive power.

============================================================ 4. MAIN
LOBBY AND MENU LAYOUT
============================================================

The lobby is a 3D fantasy environment, not a flat menu background. It
should contain a detailed character display, atmospheric sky, floating
islands, distant castles or kingdoms, waterfalls, platforms,
environmental movement and ambient sound.

Main lobby access: - Adventure World - CS - Battle Royale - Siege -
Store - Luck Royale - Events - Profile - Settings - Mail - Friends / Add
Players - World Chat - Vault / Armory - Emotes - HUD Editor - Quests and
achievements - Party and matchmaking - Pass and privilege information

The lobby should keep important actions easy to find. Avoid filling the
screen with overlapping promotional panels. Events and limited-time
offers should be visible without blocking normal play.

Store: displays purchasable cosmetics, bundles and permitted convenience
items. Google Play billing is a separate purchase bridge. The server
verifies successful transactions before granting goods.

Luck Royale: contains three custom spin categories. Each category needs
its own prize pool, odds disclosure, cost, duplicate handling and reward
history. Do not use misleading odds or hide the real cost.

Events: contains daily, weekly, seasonal, world and special challenge
activities.

Profile: displays name, level, rank information, character appearance,
statistics, achievements, records, titles and selected showcase items.

Settings: graphics, sound, music, voice, controls, sensitivity,
language, notifications, privacy, account and accessibility settings.

Mail: system notices, compensation, event rewards and approved player
messages. Attachments need expiry rules and claim history.

Friends: friend requests, party invitations, block/report tools and
online status controls.

Vault / Armory: owned weapons, armor, clothing, cosmetics, materials,
collectibles and loadouts.

World Chat: moderated communication with mute, block, report and spam
controls.

HUD Editor: lets players reposition, resize and adjust permitted
controls. Provide reset and save layouts.

============================================================ 5. PLAYER
PROGRESSION ============================================================

The player begins at Level 0. Adventure World is the beginner and main
discovery route before Level 10.

The proposed level progression structure should support long-term growth
without forcing endless repetitive grinding. The final maximum level is
not yet fixed. A scalable level range can be selected after progression
and server-economy testing.

Each level may grant: - Base character growth or attribute points,
according to the final stat design. - A level-up reward. - Access to
quests, regions, systems or activities when requirements are met. -
Occasional cosmetic, currency or material rewards. - A record entry for
milestone levels.

Do not let level alone determine every fight. Equipment, skill choices,
player execution, party coordination and creature mechanics should
matter.

Progression categories: - Character level - Equipment progression -
Weapon mastery - Skill mastery - Crafting and gathering mastery -
Exploration completion - Dungeon achievements - Competitive ranks -
Reputation or faction standing, if adopted - God inheritance
progression - Collection completion - Seasonal progression

Level 10 unlock milestone: - Access eligibility for CS, BR and Siege. -
Access to the relevant post-beginner world progression, subject to the
required Village-to-City/Kingdom pass. - Additional systems and
activities as defined by the final unlock table.

The game should clearly show locked content, its requirements and the
reason it is locked.

============================================================ 6.
ADVENTURE WORLD: LEARN AND DISCOVER
============================================================

Adventure World is the main exploration and progression experience. It
is the only primary world route available before Level 10.

The world is divided into village, city and kingdom progression. Village
instances can be numbered, for example Village 001, Village 002 and so
on, to distribute players and maintain performance. Instances should
feel like parts of one world rather than unrelated matchmaking rooms.

The player can: - Explore terrain and settlements. - Accept quests from
NPCs. - Hunt beasts and monsters. - Gather resources. - Discover caves,
ruins and hidden locations. - Find treasure and resource nodes. - Enter
available dungeons. - Meet other players and form parties. - Learn
crafting and equipment systems. - Participate in eligible events.

Village, City and Kingdom: - Village is the beginner region. - City is a
later progression area with the specified access-pass requirement. -
Kingdom is a higher-level world progression area, also governed by the
pass and level rules. - Each area should have distinctive terrain,
creatures, NPCs, resources, landmarks and challenges. - The terrain
should be reusable and expandable with additional infrastructure,
settlements, roads, vegetation and points of interest.

World instances should support dynamic transfers into inns, shops,
events, taverns, parties, dungeons, boss areas and training areas. Solo
and party instances should be supported where appropriate.

============================================================ 7.
CREATURES, SPECIES AND BEAST HUNTING
============================================================

Creatures should exist across land, air, sea, crawling and other fantasy
creature forms. Each species needs a recognizable silhouette, anatomy,
movement style, habitat, behaviour, attacks, resistances, weaknesses,
loot table and level range.

The beginner monster design requirement is two species for each level
from Level 1 to Level 10: twenty beginner species in total. Their
habitats and spawn locations should make progression understandable.
Level 1 creatures should be found close to safe beginner routes;
higher-level creatures should generally be farther away or in more
dangerous locations.

Creature families may include: - Beasts and predators - Insects and
crawling creatures - Reptilian creatures - Flying creatures - Aquatic
creatures - Elemental creatures - Undead creatures - Demonic creatures -
Magical or corrupted creatures - Boss-class creatures

Each creature record should define: - Species name and unique ID -
Creature grade - Level and level range - Habitat and spawn conditions -
Health, damage, defence and movement - Attack patterns and special
abilities - Aggression and detection range - Party scaling rules -
Respawn rules - Loot table and drop probabilities - Experience reward -
Quest and record tags - Audio, animation and visual effects

Creature behaviour: - Passive creatures may flee or defend themselves. -
Aggressive creatures may pursue players within a defined range. - Pack
creatures may coordinate. - Elite creatures should use stronger
mechanics. - Bosses should have phases, telegraphed attacks and unique
mechanics. - Flying, aquatic and crawling creatures need movement and
navigation suited to their anatomy.

Do not make every creature a simple damage sponge. Give species
different ways to attack, escape, defend, ambush or support other
creatures.

============================================================ 8. BEAST
LOOT: GRADES, CONTENTS AND DROP RULES
============================================================

Loot should be generated from a server-authorized loot table. A
creature’s grade, level, species, location, event modifiers and party
rules determine what it can drop.

PROPOSED ITEM GRADE FRAMEWORK

1.  Common Typical contents: basic hide, bone, meat, claws, scales,
    low-grade crafting materials and small amounts of Aether where the
    economy permits. Purpose: everyday crafting, cooking, beginner
    quests and basic upgrades.

2.  Uncommon Typical contents: improved hide, refined bone, quality
    scales, stronger monster parts, uncommon herbs or crafting
    components. Purpose: intermediate equipment crafting and improved
    consumables.

3.  Rare Typical contents: rare organs, special crystals, monster cores,
    rare crafting components, weapon or armor components and occasional
    equipment. Purpose: meaningful progression and specialist crafting.

4.  Epic Typical contents: powerful creature cores, enchanted materials,
    unique crafting ingredients, special equipment pieces and rare
    cosmetic components. Purpose: high-level crafting and valuable
    collection goals.

5.  Legendary Typical contents: named creature materials, legendary
    crafting components, distinctive equipment, rare collectibles and
    special titles or achievement-linked rewards. Purpose: major hunting
    milestones and endgame progression.

6.  Mythic Typical contents: extremely scarce boss materials, unique
    named equipment, exceptional crafting components and special
    collection rewards. Purpose: world-class achievements and highly
    difficult content.

7.  Divine / Inheritance Reserved for exceptional god-related content,
    divine inheritance progression and specific world-story rewards. It
    should not be a normal random drop from ordinary beasts.

These grades are a proposed framework. The final game may rename or
consolidate them after the economy and item catalogue are tested.

LOOT CATEGORIES

-   Creature materials: hide, fur, scales, bones, claws, fangs,
    feathers, shells, venom and organs.
-   Magical materials: cores, essence, crystals, elemental fragments and
    corrupted energy.
-   Consumables: food ingredients, potions and temporary buffs where
    permitted.
-   Equipment: weapons, armor, accessories and upgrade components.
-   Crafting recipes: recipes, patterns and schematics.
-   Collectibles: trophies, creature records, display items and rare
    curiosities.
-   Currency: Aether or other explicitly approved currencies.
-   Quest items: objective-specific drops that should not be
    accidentally sold or destroyed.

DROP RULES

-   A creature should have a guaranteed or near-guaranteed baseline
    material drop where appropriate.
-   Higher-grade items should have lower drop chances.
-   Bosses should use separate and clearly defined loot tables.
-   Quest drops should have protection against unreasonable bad luck.
-   Party loot rules must be visible before a boss encounter.
-   Personal loot is recommended for important boss rewards to reduce
    disputes.
-   If shared loot is used, the system must define ownership, roll
    rules, distribution and timeout.
-   A player should not receive loot if they did not meet the
    participation requirements.
-   Anti-bot and anti-exploit checks must prevent repeated automated
    farming.
-   Drop odds should be testable and auditable by the server.
-   Event bonuses must not secretly multiply rare drops beyond published
    rules.

PROPOSED DROP-TABLE EXAMPLE

Ordinary low-level beast: - Common materials: frequent. - Uncommon
materials: occasional. - Rare materials: very low chance. - Epic and
above: not normally available.

Elite beast: - Common and uncommon materials: regular. - Rare materials:
possible. - Epic materials: low chance. - Legendary and above: normally
unavailable unless specifically designed.

Dungeon boss: - Guaranteed boss token or core fragment, if the dungeon
rules specify it. - Several grade-appropriate materials. - Chance at
equipment or a recipe. - First-clear reward handled separately from
repeat loot.

World boss: - Participation reward. - Boss-specific materials. - Chance
at rare named equipment or collectible. - Server-wide record and event
contribution rewards.

Exact percentages, item quantities and pity protection must be set per
creature and dungeon. They should not be invented globally because a
single drop percentage would distort the entire economy.

============================================================ 9. ITEM
EQUIPMENT AND CHARACTER BUILD
============================================================

Equipment categories: - Main-hand weapons - Off-hand weapons or shields,
where supported - Dual-wield combinations - Head armor - Chest armor -
Arm armor - Leg armor - Foot armor - Accessories - Cosmetic clothing and
appearance items

Weapons should support different combat styles: swords, blades, axes,
spears, bows, magical weapons and other original fantasy weapon types.

The dual-wield system should have a dedicated toggle where the weapon
combination supports it. Each weapon type needs its own animation,
attack timing, range, effects and mastery progression.

Equipment should have: - Item name and ID - Grade - Level requirement -
Class or weapon compatibility if classes are used - Base attributes -
Durability rules, if durability is adopted - Upgrade level - Enhancement
limits - Socket or rune rules, if adopted - Binding and trading status -
Source and acquisition history

Avoid making grade the only measure of usefulness. A lower-grade item
with a suitable build or special effect may remain useful in certain
situations.

============================================================ 10. PERKS,
SKILLS AND BUILDS
============================================================

The skill system should allow different builds without creating one
mandatory combination.

Proposed perk categories: - Offensive: attack power, critical chance,
weapon mastery and damage specialization. - Defensive: health, defence,
resistance, blocking and damage reduction. - Mobility: dodge recovery,
movement and traversal improvements. - Survival: resource efficiency,
recovery and environmental resistance. - Beast hunting: tracking,
harvesting efficiency and creature-specific knowledge. - Crafting:
material efficiency, recipe access and crafting quality. - Party
support: healing, protection, buffs and coordination. - Elemental:
bonuses tied to elemental abilities or equipment. - Utility:
interaction, gathering and other non-combat improvements.

Perks must have clear descriptions, requirements, costs and effects.
Avoid stacking effects without limits. Define caps, diminishing returns
and mutually exclusive choices where necessary.

Skills: - Each skill needs an ID, name, icon, animation, effect,
cooldown, cost, range, targeting rules and upgrade path. - Skills must
work with mobile controls. - Skill effects should be readable in
multiplayer combat. - Bosses should not be permanently disabled by
repeated crowd control. - Balance must consider solo and party play.

The final number of skill slots and skill trees should be established
through the combat prototype rather than guessed.

============================================================ 11.
DUNGEONS AND INSTANCE CONTENT
============================================================

Dungeons are structured PvE challenges with enemies, mechanics, rewards
and completion records.

Dungeon types may include: - Solo dungeons - Party dungeons - Challenge
dungeons - Timed dungeons - Boss lairs - Story dungeons - Event
dungeons - Endgame dungeons

Every dungeon should define: - Entry location and unlock requirements -
Recommended level and party size - Entry limits or cooldowns, if used -
Difficulty selection - Dungeon map and room sequence - Enemy groups and
elite encounters - Traps, puzzles or environmental mechanics - Boss
mechanics and phases - Completion conditions - Failure and restart
rules - Loot table - First-clear reward - Repeat-clear reward - Time and
performance records - Anti-exploit and disconnect handling

DUNGEON FLOW

1.  Player discovers or unlocks the dungeon.
2.  Player reads the requirements and reward preview.
3.  Player enters alone or forms a party.
4.  The instance loads the dungeon layout and encounter state.
5.  Players clear enemies, solve mechanics and defeat bosses.
6.  The server validates the result.
7.  Rewards are granted and the record is saved.
8.  The party receives a clear summary showing time, deaths,
    contribution and rewards.

FIRST-CLEAR REWARDS

The first successful clear of a dungeon should provide a special
one-time reward, such as: - First-clear chest - Dungeon title or badge -
Unique trophy - Aether or progression currency - Crafting recipe -
Dungeon-specific material - Achievement record

First-clear rewards must be account-bound or character-bound where
necessary to prevent players from repeatedly exploiting new characters
or accounts. The exact scope—per character, account or server—must be
stated for each reward.

REPEAT-CLEAR REWARDS

Repeat clears may grant: - Standard dungeon materials - Boss tokens -
Crafting components - Equipment drops - Currency within economy limits -
Weekly or daily reward progress

Repeat rewards should remain useful without allowing unlimited farming
to break the economy.

============================================================ 12.
RECORDS, FIRST-TIME ACHIEVEMENTS AND WORLD HISTORY
============================================================

Soulbound Legends should maintain a detailed record system. Records make
major accomplishments visible and give players long-term goals.

Record categories:

FIRST-TIME RECORDS - First player to reach a level milestone on a
server. - First player to discover a named location. - First player or
party to clear a dungeon. - First player to defeat a specific boss. -
First player to defeat a world boss, where the event permits a single
credited first. - First player to craft a named high-grade item. - First
player to discover a rare creature. - First player to complete a major
quest chain. - First player to claim an eligible God inheritance. -
First party to complete a special challenge. - First player to achieve a
defined mastery milestone.

COMBAT RECORDS - Total beasts defeated. - Species defeated. - Elite
beasts defeated. - Bosses defeated. - Highest damage in a valid
encounter. - Most damage blocked or mitigated. - Healing and support
contribution. - Longest survival streak. - Dungeon deaths and successful
no-death clears.

EXPLORATION RECORDS - Regions discovered. - Landmarks discovered. -
Hidden locations found. - Dungeons unlocked. - Treasure discoveries. -
Exploration completion percentage.

CRAFTING AND COLLECTION RECORDS - Items crafted. - Highest-grade item
crafted. - Recipes discovered. - Materials gathered. - Collections
completed. - Rare trophies obtained.

COMPETITIVE RECORDS - CS rank history. - BR rank history. - Siege rank
history. - Wins, losses and performance. - Seasonal placements. -
Tournament achievements if tournaments are introduced.

WORLD RECORDS - Rift events completed. - World bosses defeated. -
Inheritances claimed. - Major world milestones. - Barrier progression. -
Server-wide discoveries.

REWARDING RECORDS

Each record should have a reward tier based on difficulty and
importance: - Minor record: achievement points, small currency or a
basic title. - Notable record: title, badge, materials or cosmetic. -
Major record: exclusive title, trophy, rare cosmetic or substantial
progression reward. - Historic first: permanent history entry, unique
title or badge, display trophy and an appropriate one-time reward.

A first-time reward must never be granted repeatedly by resetting a
character. The server should define whether the record is global,
server-wide, account-wide, character-specific, seasonal or repeatable.

Records should store: - Player or party identity - Server identifier -
Record category - Achievement ID - Timestamp - Relevant encounter or
activity ID - Validation status - Reward claimed status - Whether it is
a first-ever, first-on-server or personal best

A leaderboard should distinguish legitimate records and should not
reward exploits, AFK participation or manipulated matchmaking.

============================================================ 13. QUESTS
AND NPC SYSTEM
============================================================

Quest types: - Main story quests - Beginner/tutorial quests - Side
quests - Daily quests - Weekly quests - Hunting contracts - Gathering
quests - Dungeon quests - Party quests - Exploration quests - Event
quests - Hidden discovery quests

Quest data should include objectives, location, NPC, level requirement,
prerequisites, rewards, expiry, repeat rules and completion validation.

NPCs should have: - Identity and role - Appearance and animation -
Dialogue - Voice configuration - Daily or event schedules where
supported - Quest availability - Shop or service access - Navigation and
interaction logic - Faction or relationship state if adopted

NPC generation should use controlled templates and rules. Random
generation must not create broken quests, impossible dialogue or invalid
rewards.

============================================================ 14. THE
THREE COMPETITIVE MODES
============================================================

All three modes unlock at Level 10 under the established progression
rule. Each has a separate rank progression.

A. CS — PRACTICE & SHARPEN

CS is an elimination-focused competitive mode. It is not a site-capture
mode.

-   Teams compete through rounds.
-   Elimination and round victory determine the result.
-   Five broad map environments: grasslands, ruins, desert, snow and
    ocean/air.
-   The planned map catalogue contains 35 submaps.
-   Competitive matchmaking should consider rank, performance, party
    size and connection quality.
-   CS rank is separate from BR and Siege rank.

B. BR — ADAPT & SURVIVE

-   Large fantasy maps.
-   Five separate large-scale map concepts.
-   Players enter through a random light-beam teleport rather than an
    airplane.
-   Players search for equipment, adapt to threats and survive.
-   The HUD includes alive-player and kill information.
-   The final safe-zone, match duration, squad size and loot balance
    need dedicated tuning.
-   BR rank is separate from CS and Siege rank.

C. SIEGE — COORDINATE & EXECUTE

-   Teams attack and defend a core.
-   The planned structure uses best-of-two rounds followed by a final
    simultaneous-objective stage, subject to final rules and map
    testing.
-   The planned map catalogue contains 35 submaps.
-   Team coordination and objective execution matter.
-   Siege rank is separate from CS and BR rank.

Competitive rewards may include: - Rank points - Seasonal currency -
Rank badges - Titles - Character cosmetics - Weapon cosmetics - Emotes -
Profile frames - Seasonal trophies

Rank rewards should emphasize skill and participation, not direct
purchase of competitive advantage.

============================================================ 15. RIFT
EVENTS AND SUPREME RUINS
============================================================

Rifts appear around cities and kingdoms. They are world events that can
introduce dangerous enemies, special objectives, rare materials and
story discoveries.

A Rift event should define: - Spawn conditions - Location and warning
period - Recommended level - Participation rules - Enemy waves or
objectives - Boss encounter - Contribution tracking - Completion and
failure conditions - Personal and group rewards - Event record and
history

Supreme Ruins are major inheritance locations. They should be
exceptionally important and carefully controlled.

-   A Supreme Ruin provides a route to one God inheritance.
-   Only one challenger can attempt the relevant challenge at a time.
-   The current holder cannot challenge for another inheritance or
    assist another challenger under the established restriction.
-   The challenge must have clear entry, failure, completion and reward
    rules.
-   The server must validate ownership and prevent duplicate claims.
-   The world record should show the inheritance holder and relevant
    claim history.

============================================================ 16. PLAYER
KILLING, WANTED STATUS AND PK
============================================================

Player killing has consequences.

Established direction: - PK can cause a red-name status. - Players can
receive a loot bounty under the applicable rules. - PK levels track
escalating behaviour. - At PK Level 10, the player becomes wanted. -
Later world-story progression introduces a demon-war choice and
God-chosen PK inheritance rules.

The final PK system must define: - What counts as an unlawful attack. -
Safe-zone protections. - Self-defence exceptions. - Party and guild
exceptions if guilds are introduced. - Red-name duration and removal
conditions. - Bounty calculation and collection. - What loot can be
dropped. - Protected items that cannot be looted. - Wanted-player
tracking and hunting rules. - Anti-griefing safeguards. - How accidental
attacks are handled.

Do not allow unrestricted looting of purchased goods, account-bound
rewards or essential progression items. The loot rules must be clearly
shown before players enter dangerous PvP areas.

============================================================ 17.
CURRENCY, STORE, AUCTION AND ECONOMY
============================================================

Aether is the named in-game currency.

The economy should distinguish: - Earnable gameplay currency. - Premium
purchase currency, if introduced. - Event currencies. - Dungeon or boss
tokens. - Crafting materials. - Account-bound rewards. - Tradable goods.

The Google Play purchase bridge is separate from the in-game auction and
shops. Purchases must be verified by the server before granting items.

The auction and commerce system must use server authorization. It should
support: - Listing items - Search and filters - Price display - Listing
fees, if adopted - Transaction history - Delivery and claim -
Cancellation rules - Anti-fraud checks - Trade restrictions - Price
manipulation detection

The game should have currency sinks, such as approved crafting,
services, upgrades or listing fees, to prevent uncontrolled inflation.
All prices and reward rates need economy simulation before launch.

============================================================ 18. AUDIO,
VOICE AND COMMUNICATION
============================================================

Audio systems include: - Background music - Lobby music - Exploration
ambience - Combat music - Boss music - Dungeon ambience - Environmental
sound - Weapon and ability sound - Creature calls and attacks - UI sound
effects - Voice chat

Voice settings should include a clear slider arrangement for the
intended masculine/feminine voice presentation, with the right/left
direction as specified in the design. NPC voice selection should
correspond to the NPC’s configured gender presentation. The final
implementation must use licensed or original voice assets.

Voice chat needs: - Mute and unmute - Speaker controls - Microphone
controls - Party and proximity rules if proximity chat is adopted -
Report and moderation tools - Privacy settings

============================================================ 19. MOBILE
HUD AND CONTROLS
============================================================

The HUD should use a clean black-focused button language with original
fantasy-themed icons and visual assets. It must not copy another game’s
exact layout or artwork.

Core HUD: - Minimap at the top-left. - Health and mana indicators. -
Microphone and speaker controls near the crosshair area. - Movement
joystick. - Swipe-based dodge in up, left, right and down directions. -
Left-hand and right-hand attack controls. - Eight-slot skill/emote
wheel. - Interaction button slightly right of centre. - Weapon switching
bar. - Dual-wield toggle where applicable. - BR alive-player and kill
panel.

The HUD must support: - Repositioning and resizing. - Sensitivity
options. - Button opacity and scale. - Save and reset layouts. -
Different device aspect ratios. - Accessibility and touch-target
requirements.

The same core HUD language should be used across modes, with
mode-specific information added where necessary.

============================================================ 20.
CHARACTER, MONSTER, CLOTHING AND WORLD GENERATION
============================================================

Character generation should support controlled customization and
consistent animation. It should include body, head, hair, eyes, ears,
clothing, armor, accessories and equipment attachment points.

Monster generation should support land, air, sea, crawling and other
creature forms. A generator must not simply produce random disconnected
parts. It needs valid anatomy rules, skeleton compatibility, locomotion,
attack sets, collision, animation and performance limits.

World generation and map assembly should use reusable terrain and
modular infrastructure. The terrain should be separable from roads,
vegetation, houses, buildings and other structures so that the same
terrain foundation can be expanded.

The world system should support: - Terrain - Roads and paths -
Vegetation - Buildings - Settlements - Castles and infrastructure -
Dungeons - Resource nodes - Creature spawn areas - Navigation data -
Lighting and atmosphere - Audio zones - Streaming and level-of-detail
rules

============================================================ 21. SERVER,
SECURITY AND LIVE OPERATIONS
============================================================

Core Server Authorization controls important game operations. Soulbound
Sentinel is the additional security and monitoring layer intended to
cover gaps.

Core server responsibilities: - Authentication and account data - Player
progression - Inventory and item ownership - Currency - Purchases -
Auction and trade - Loot generation - Match results - Dungeon
completion - Records and achievements - Inheritance ownership - Event
rewards

Soulbound Sentinel direction: - Monitor online players, accounts,
economy, APIs and servers. - Detect suspicious behaviour and
tampering. - Maintain incidents, investigations, policies and audit
logs. - Support Super Admin, Security Admin, Auditor and Read-Only
Monitor roles. - Produce a While You Were Away report. - Include a
game-link field. - Apply a documented punishment ladder. - Keep an audit
trail for administrator actions.

Security must be server-authoritative. The client must not decide its
own currency, loot, purchases, rank results or inheritance ownership.

============================================================ 22. CONTENT
UPDATES AND LIVE SERVICE
============================================================

The game should be designed so that future content can be updated
without rebuilding every existing system.

Editable content should include: - Items and equipment - Skills and
perks - Monsters and NPCs - Quests - Maps and regions - Dungeons and
bosses - Events and Rift events - Store and Luck Royale pools - UI
content - Progression requirements - Drop tables - Audio and music -
Balance values - Server rules

Updates should be versioned, tested and reversible. A failed content
update must not corrupt player inventories or progression.

The initial launch should contain the agreed core experience. Later
updates can expand endgame content, major story revelations, finishing
content and additional systems as players progress.

============================================================ 23. REWARD
DESIGN PRINCIPLES
============================================================

Rewards should match the difficulty, time and risk of the activity.

Examples: - Beginner quests: experience, basic materials, starter
equipment and guidance. - Ordinary beasts: common materials and
occasional uncommon drops. - Elite beasts: better materials and
rare-drop opportunities. - Dungeon completion: dungeon materials, tokens
and equipment chances. - First dungeon clear: one-time first-clear
reward and permanent record. - World boss: contribution-based reward,
boss materials and event record. - First discovery: discovery record,
title or collectible where appropriate. - Competitive seasons:
rank-based cosmetics, badges and seasonal currency. - Major story
milestones: unique titles, trophies and world-history entries. - God
inheritance: unique world-changing progression and permanent ownership
record.

Reward safeguards: - Do not reward AFK players as if they contributed. -
Do not let repeated account resets claim first-time rewards. - Show
reward requirements before the activity. - Save reward grants in a
transaction-safe way. - Keep a claim history. - Provide support tools
for missing or incorrectly granted rewards.

============================================================ 26. THE
INTENDED PLAYER JOURNEY
============================================================

A player begins by witnessing the ancient divine catastrophe. They
create an account and character, enter the lobby and learn the controls.
They start at Level 0 and enter Adventure World, where they explore a
beginner village, meet NPCs, complete quests, gather resources and fight
the first creatures.

As the player grows, they discover new species, collect materials of
different grades, craft and improve equipment, learn skills and perks,
find hidden places and enter increasingly challenging content.

At Level 10, the player reaches a major progression milestone. The
competitive modes become available under the game’s access rules, and
the player can pursue CS, BR and Siege ranks while continuing the
Adventure World journey and qualifying for later regions through the
required pass.

Further progression introduces cities, kingdoms, stronger beasts, elite
encounters, dungeons, world bosses, Rift events and Supreme Ruins.
Players build reputations through records, first clears, discoveries,
crafting, competition and major world events.

The world’s divine history gradually becomes clearer. God inheritances
become central to the world’s future. The barrier’s eventual fall is
governed by the established world conditions. The hidden Shadow God’s
betrayal remains a major mystery until the intended story reveal.

The result should be a world where players have reasons to explore,
hunt, craft, compete, cooperate, collect, improve and return—not merely
grind levels.
